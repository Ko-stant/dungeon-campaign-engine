Hero Quest · house rules for our table

Players' Handbook

Our quests use new rules: real dice instead of skulls and shields, bigger Body pools, abilities you'll use every fight, and monsters that hit hard. This is everything you need at the table. The rest you'll find out in the dark.

What's new

Changes since you last played HeroQuest.

Roll to hit

You roll your class's hit dice against the monster's Avoidance. Hit it and you deal your damage, every time.

Fixed damage

Damage comes from your class and your gear. A better weapon means bigger hits.

Crits and near misses

A separate d20 crit die doubles a hit, or can turn a near miss into a hit.

Big Body pools

Heroes have 28 to 40 Body and monsters have far more than before. Most fights last a few rounds.

Abilities every fight

Each hero has abilities on cooldowns (the Cleric spends mana). Using them is the point; the question is when.

No bad luck streaks

Every miss in a row makes your next attack more accurate, and badly hurt monsters are easier to finish.

Your turn

  • On your turn you move and take one action, in either order: attack, use an ability, cast a spell, search, disarm a trap.
  • A round is one full cycle: every hero and every monster takes a turn. Cooldowns are counted in rounds.
  • Movement is rolled as before (2d6 for every hero to start).
  • Drinking a potion is free: any time, even in the middle of the monsters' turn, between two attacks.
  • Some abilities say free (no action needed) or reaction (used on the monsters' turn, when something happens to you).

Attacking

  1. Roll your hit dice and the d20 crit die together. Read them separately.
  2. Add your Accuracy to the hit dice. If the total meets or beats the monster's Avoidance, you hit.
  3. A hit deals your damage in full. Monsters have no damage mitigation.
  4. If the crit die lands in your crit range:
    • on a hit, your damage is doubled;
    • on a miss, add +2 to your hit total. If that reaches the Avoidance, it's a hit for normal damage (a near miss).
Example

Papi (hit dice 2d10, Accuracy +2, crit 15-20, damage 6) attacks a monster with Avoidance 14. He rolls 5 and 6 on the hit dice and 17 on the crit die. 11 + 2 = 13 misses by one, but the crit die is in his range, so +2 makes it 15: a hit for 6 damage. Had the hit dice reached 14 on their own, the crit would have doubled it to 12.

Who you can hit

Your normal attack reaches different squares depending on your class. Abilities say their own reach (Fan of Cards hits every enemy around you, diagonals included).

Barbarian

Adjacent or diagonal. The weapon is big enough to reach the corners.

Ranger

Anything in line of sight, including adjacent and diagonal squares. Unlike the old crossbow, you can shoot an enemy right next to you.

Rogue and Cleric

Adjacent only, not diagonal. The Cleric's Smite is a spell with its own range.

Critical miss

Every die shows 1 (all hit dice and the crit die). You miss and lose your next turn. Very rare.

Special crit

Every die shows its highest face. A crit, with a flourish worth telling: something spectacular happens.

Determination

Each miss in a row gives you +2 Accuracy on your next attack, up to +4. Any hit resets it. Keep a token or die by your card.

Sure hit

Some abilities and a monster that can't defend mean you roll only the crit die: it can't miss, and a crit still doubles.

Being attacked

  1. The monster rolls its hit dice.
  2. You roll your defense dice and add your avoidance (class plus gear).
  3. The monster hits only if it beats your total. Ties go to you.
  4. You take its damage minus your mitigation. Enough mitigation can bring a hit to 0.
  • Avoidance keeps you from being hit; mitigation softens the hits that land.
  • Monsters can crit too: they also roll 2d20, and double 20s double their damage (before your mitigation).
  • If you can't defend (some abilities do this to you), you roll no defense dice, but your avoidance still counts.
  • A hero who reaches 0 Body falls. Keep each other alive: potions are free to drink and the Cleric can heal.

Mind and magic

Mind is your Will. Quick feet can dodge a blade, but not a curse.

  • Some attacks are mental: spells, curses, fear, the wail of the restless dead. The monster's card says which.
  • Against a mental attack you roll your defense dice + Mind instead of defense dice + avoidance. Your avoidance doesn't count. The monster must beat your total, and ties go to you.
  • They rarely cost Body. Instead they leave a condition the TV shows on your card, such as Frightened (you can't attack that monster on your next turn), Dazed (you lose your next action) or Cursed (-2 Accuracy for 2 rounds).
  • The Cleric has the strongest Will in the party; the Barbarian, the weakest. Look after each other.
  • The Cleric's faith drives Smite too: it rolls 1d20 + Mind to hit.

Monsters

You won't know a monster's numbers until you meet it. When you do, the Chronicler can show its card on the TV with everything listed below.

Avoidance

The number your hit roll must reach. Always even. Weak monsters are easy to hit; the worst of them are not.

Body

How much damage it takes to kill. Big monsters take several hits from the whole party.

Hit dice and damage

What it rolls against your defense, and how hard it hits when it gets through.

Wounded

A monster at a quarter of its maximum Body or less (rounded down) is faltering: its Avoidance drops by 4, so anyone can land the finishing blow.

Traits

Some monsters have special traits. The TV card lists them like this:

ranged

Attacks from a distance. Holding a doorway won't keep it off you.

reach

Strikes past the hero in front: each attack also hits the hero standing behind its target, in a straight line, so a doorway won't shelter the one behind. Each hero defends separately.

AoE

Its attack also deals light damage to heroes near the one it targets. Don't bunch up.

undead

Smite deals extra damage to it.

  • Facing: a monster faces the hero it last attacked. The square on the opposite side is behind it (the Rogue loves this).
  • Some monsters have abilities of their own, written on their card.

Fights, cooldowns and mana

  • The Chronicler says when a fight starts and ends.
  • An ability's cooldown is how many rounds until you can use it again. Used in round 4 with cooldown 3, it's ready in round 7.
  • When a fight ends, any ability still on cooldown drops to 1 or 2 rounds left and waits there until the next fight:
    • cooldown 1: ready again;
    • cooldown 2-3: drops to 1 round left;
    • cooldown 4 or more: drops to 2 rounds left. If it only had 1 left, it stays at 1.
  • Example: Mordecai uses Aimed Shot (cooldown 5) and the fight ends with 4 rounds still to go. It drops to 2 and stays there until the next fight, where it's ready after 2 rounds. Had it been down to 1, it would stay at 1. An ability you didn't use stays ready.
  • The Cleric's mana partly regenerates at the start of the heroes' turn during a fight. Out of a fight, only Prayer restores it, so healing up between fights leaves less for the next one.
  • Abilities are meant to be used. The choice is when, not whether.

Gear, gold and potions

  • Your gear is your numbers. Weapons add damage. Most armor adds avoidance (you're harder to hit); heavy armor adds mitigation (hits hurt less). Rare pieces add Accuracy or mana.
  • Only equipped items count. You keep your old gear when you find something new.
  • Class rules: only the Barbarian uses two-handed weapons and heavy armor; the Rogue always fights with two weapons; only the Ranger uses bows and crossbows; only the Cleric casts spells and carries a shield.
  • Gold is shared by the whole party: one purse.
  • A healing potion restores 8 Body; a mana potion 6 mana. Drinking is free.

The TV

The Chronicler resolves things on the table, then updates the app while you decide what to do. The TV follows along live and only ever shows what your heroes know.

The map

The whole dungeon's walls are drawn. Rooms you've explored turn light; monsters, furniture and doors appear once you can see them.

The party

Everyone's Body, Mind and mana, and effects like Raging or Poisoned with rounds left.

Cards

Ask the Chronicler to show any monster or hero on the map: its stats, traits and what's affecting it.

What happened

A log of the last few things that happened, if you lose track. Traps and secrets never appear there.

Glossary

Accuracy
A flat bonus added to your hit dice.
Adjacent
The four squares sharing a side with yours. Diagonal is the four corner squares; around you means all eight.
Armor
Worn gear, not a stat. Most armor adds avoidance; heavy armor adds mitigation.
Avoidance
For a monster, the number your hit roll must reach. For you, the flat part of your defense (class plus gear): it keeps you from being hit.
Cooldown
Rounds until an ability can be used again. When a fight ends it drops to 1 or 2 rounds left and waits for the next fight.
Crit range
The d20 results that crit for you: double damage on a hit, +2 on a miss.
Defense dice
What you roll, plus your avoidance, when a monster attacks you.
Determination
+2 Accuracy for each miss in a row, up to +4; any hit resets it.
Fight
From when the Chronicler says it starts until it's over. Mana regenerates and cooldowns finish only in fights.
Mind
Your Will: defense dice + Mind is your defense against mental attacks. The Cleric's Smite rolls 1d20 + Mind.
Mitigation
Taken off every hit you suffer. Can bring a hit to 0. Monsters have none.
Round
One full cycle in which every hero and monster takes a turn.
Sure hit
No hit roll: only the crit die is rolled.
Wounded
A monster at a quarter of its maximum Body or less, rounded down: Avoidance -4.
