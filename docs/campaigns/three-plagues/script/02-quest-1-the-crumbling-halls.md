# The Three Plagues - Script: Quest 1 - The Crumbling Halls

**Last Updated**: 2026-10-03 16:01 EDT

Part of the read-aloud script; see [README.md](README.md) for the format and how to load it.
Everything above the `##` heading is left out when the script is assembled.

The heroes know only what Lord Voss told them. The notes in this quest (Q1-N1 to Q1-N3)
are the first traces of the parties who came before: they show that people died here,
that something worse waits in the Eastmarch, and that the Chapel pilgrims went on toward
a place called the Rise. The GM places each note in a room of their choice; the heroes find
it when they search there.

### Board checklist (for the Crumbling Halls quest in the map creator)

Each find gets a quest note on its square, so it shows on the board and in the tracker's
Quest notes list (Mark used once found). Start the note text with the passage id.

      tomb: verse, last two lines scratched out".

      Tomas Reed: remains under the fall, note in his helmet". Aldo lies under the same

## Quest 1 - The Crumbling Halls

### Q1-01 - The Crumbling Halls

- **When:** the quest begins.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** hushed, echoing; let the pauses breathe.
- **Sound:** dripping water, trickling dust, distant falling stone.

> The great doors groan shut behind you, and the dark closes in.
>
> The Wardens built this gatehouse to last forever. It is dying anyway. Every step you
> take shakes loose a trickle of dust. Pillars lean like tired old men. Whole passages
> have fallen in, and others, sealed for centuries, have cracked open to the air. The
> stone itself is weakening, as if the earth has forgotten how to hold its shape.
>
> Others have come this way before you. Somewhere in these dark halls, some of them, or at
> least their bones, are still here. Whatever has made its home down here will not welcome
> you either. Somewhere on the far side is the eastern gate, and beyond it, the Eastmarch.

**Quest goals**
- Cross the Crumbling Halls and leave by the eastern gate.
- *(Optional)* Search for anything the lost parties, or the Wardens, left behind.

### Q1-02 - The Warden's verse

- **When:** the heroes search the room where the GM placed a Warden's tomb (or any wall
  carving). Clue 2.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** slower; read the two surviving lines plainly, almost flat, like words cut in
  stone. Pause before each lone surviving word.
- **Note:** the last two lines were scratched away on purpose. Only "one" (line 3) and
  "waits" (line 4) survive. The full verse is in the story bible, for the GM only.

> Carved into the lid of a Warden's tomb, half buried in rubble, are four signs you have
> seen before, on the cracked fountain at Voss Keep. A mountain. A wave. A feather. A flame.
>
> Beneath them runs a verse, cut deep and careful, as if whoever carved it wanted it never
> to be forgotten.
>
> Three the plagues that bar the way.\
> Three the stones that turn the key.
>
> The last two lines are gone. Not worn away by time. Gouged out, stroke after stroke,
> by something sharp, until the stone beneath is scarred and pale. Someone wanted these
> words forgotten after all.
>
> Of the third line, a single word has survived, near the middle: one.
>
> Of the last line, only the final word can still be read: waits.

### Q1-N1 - The patrol's last watch

- **When:** the heroes search the room where the GM placed a collapsed passage and a
  soldier's remains. The note is tucked inside a dented Keep-guard helmet.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Tomas Reed.
- **Voice (Tomas):** a young soldier, hoarse and exhausted, trying to stay brave for
  whoever reads it. He gets quieter toward the end.
- **Sound:** a slow drip; the creak of settling stone.

**Narrator**

> Half buried under a fall of rubble lies what is left of a soldier. A faded blue surcoat,
> the same as Sergeant Hale's. His legs are pinned beneath a slab of stone, and one hand is
> still curled around a stub of charcoal. Inside his helmet, folded small, is a scrap of
> paper covered in cramped, careful writing.

**Tomas Reed**

> Tomas Reed, Keep guard. If Sergeant Hale reads this, I'm sorry.
>
> The ceiling came down in the long hall. Aldo was in front of me. I can't hear him any
> more. My legs are stuck and I can't feel them. Corporal Brandt says they'll go on to the
> far gate and come back for me on the way home. I told them to go. Somebody has to see.
>
> It's been two days, I think. Something moves in the walls when the torch gutters. It
> doesn't come close while there's light. I have one torch left.
>
> Tell my mother I wasn't afraid. Tell her I wasn't.

### Q1-N2 - A warning in red

- **When:** the heroes search the room nearest the eastern gate that the GM chooses. The
  note is pinned to a door or wall with a broken knife.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Bram Greyford.
- **Voice (Bram):** a hard, older sellsword, angry and ashamed; he spits the last lines.
- **Sound:** the knife creaking in the wood as it is pulled free.

**Narrator**

> Someone has driven a broken knife into the wood at head height, pinning a sheet of
> stained paper where no one coming this way could miss it. The words are written in
> something that has dried a dull brown, and underlined twice.

**Bram Greyford**

> To whoever comes after. Turn back.
>
> I am Bram Greyford, and I have led my company through six wars. We came east for the
> lord's gold. There were six of us. Now there are only four.
>
> The farms past this gate are wrong. The crops are swollen and split. The cattle lie in
> the fields and swell until they burst. There are gray threads strung over everything,
> fence to barn, barn to tree, and they move when nothing is touching them. Harl went into the
> big barn at the center after a sound he swore was a child crying. We heard him scream. We
> did not go in after him. Whatever sits in that barn is patient, and it is not alone.
>
> We are going home. No coin is worth this. If you have any sense, you will go home too.

### Q1-N3 - The pilgrims' mark

- **When:** the heroes search the room where the GM placed a statue. The mark is drawn on
  its base in white chalk.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Sister Wenna.
- **Voice (Wenna):** a calm, warm older woman, sure of her faith. Read it as a blessing.
- **Sound:** quiet; a single drip.

**Narrator**

> On the base of a statue, sheltered from the dust, someone has drawn in white chalk a circle cut into
> four parts. In each part is a small sign: a mountain, a wave, a feather and a flame. The
> sign of the Chapel of the Four. Beneath it, in a neat and steady hand, are a few lines of
> writing.

**Sister Wenna**

> Sister Wenna and eight of the faithful of the Chapel of the Four passed this way, on the
> third day of our journey. The Four walk with us.
>
> We seek the Wardens' fortress on the Rise, where the records say the way to the Chambers
> begins. If you are of the faith, pray for us. If you are not, follow the chalk. We have
> marked the safe path.

### Q1-03 - The eastern gate

- **When:** the heroes reach the exit.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** relief first, then unease.
- **Sound:** heavy doors; warm, heavy air; flies.

> Daylight. A thin gray line of it, spilling under a pair of iron-bound doors at the end of
> the hall. The eastern gate.
>
> As you heave the doors open, a breath of warm air rolls in to meet you. It is heavy, and
> sweet, and wrong, like fruit left far too long in the sun.
>
> Somewhere out there are the farms of the Eastmarch.
