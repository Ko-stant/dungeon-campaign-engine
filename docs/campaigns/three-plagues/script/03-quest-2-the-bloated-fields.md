# The Three Plagues - Script: Quest 2 - The Bloated Fields

**Last Updated**: 2026-10-03 16:01 EDT

Part of the read-aloud script; see [README.md](README.md) for the format and how to load it.
Everything above the `##` heading is left out when the script is assembled.

The heroes arrive in the Eastmarch knowing only Bram Greyford's warning about the barn and
Sister Wenna's mark pointing to the Rise. Here they learn a name for the thing in the barn
(from Tam, or his mother's note), and the first hint that the glowing stone inside it is a
key (Wenna's journal). The words "Soul Gem" and "plague" are never said aloud.

## Quest 2 - The Bloated Fields

### Q2-01 - The Eastmarch

- **When:** the quest begins.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** disgust held under control; slow on "like something breathing."
- **Sound:** buzzing flies, creaking fences, a wet bubbling.

> You step out of the dark into a stink so thick you can taste it.
>
> The farms of the Eastmarch stretch before you under a low, yellow sky. The wheat has
> swollen and split open on the stalk. Cattle lie in the fields, bloated and still. Fat
> gray threads drift between the hedgerows, strung fence to barn, barn to tree, and where
> they touch the ground, the earth heaves and bubbles like something breathing. The houses
> of a small village stand empty nearby, their doors webbed shut.
>
> Bram Greyford was right. These fields are wrong. And every one of those threads runs the
> same way, toward the heart of the farmland, and a great dark barn.

**Quest goals**
- Find what is poisoning the Eastmarch, and end it.
- Cross the fields and find the road onward to the Rise.
- *(Optional)* Search the village and the farms for anyone, or anything, left behind.

### Q2-02 - The boy in the cellar (optional)

- **When:** the heroes search the cellar or grain store where the GM hid Tam in Millbrook.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Tam Fenwick.
- **Voice (Tam):** a twelve-year-old, whispering, shaking, trying hard not to cry.

**Narrator**

> Behind a stack of rotten grain sacks, something moves. A boy, no more than twelve,
> clutching a pitchfork taller than he is.

**Tam**

> Don't. Don't come any closer.
>
> You're not sick? You're not webbed?
>
> Everyone went to the big tithe barn, in the middle of the fields. The threads took them
> there. Ma too. I heard her calling me. It wasn't her voice, though. Not really.
>
> It's the spider. The big one. Ma called it the Blightweaver. It sits in the middle and
> pulls on the threads, and whatever's caught comes to it.
>
> If you're going there, follow the thickest threads. And please. If you find my ma, tell
> her Tam's still hiding. Like she told me to.

### Q2-N1 - The Greyford camp

- **When:** the heroes search the farmhouse where the GM placed the Greyford Company's old
  camp. The page is weighed down by a stone beside a long-dead fire.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Lark.
- **Voice (Lark):** a young scout, quick and nervous, writing fast by firelight. She
  steadies herself on the last line.
- **Sound:** a creaking shutter; flies.

**Narrator**

> Someone camped here, a long time ago. Six bedrolls, rotted flat. A ring of blackened
> stones. Beside the fire, pinned under a flat rock, is a page torn from a journal, the ink
> faded but still clear.

**Lark**

> Lark, of the Greyford Company. Fourth night past the Halls.
>
> Bram's taken to calling this place the bloated fields. He isn't wrong. Everything here
> swells until it splits. We burned the threads around the house and they screamed as they
> burned. I swear they did.
>
> Harl is gone. He went into the big barn after the crying and didn't come out. Before the
> screaming started, I saw light through the gaps in the boards. Green light. Like a lantern
> inside something's belly.
>
> Dace touched the threads yesterday. His hand has gone gray to the wrist and he won't stop
> scratching it.
>
> Bram says we go back at first light. Nobody argued.

### Q2-N2 - The pilgrims' road

- **When:** the heroes search the wayside shrine or roadside stone the GM placed north of
  the barn. The page is tucked into a crack in the stone, beneath the Chapel's chalk sign.
  Clue 3.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Sister Wenna.
- **Voice (Wenna):** calm and warm, as in Q1-N3, but troubled now. Slow down for "three
  stones that turn the key."
- **Sound:** quiet; a far-off crow.

**Narrator**

> On a weathered roadside stone, someone has chalked the sign of the Chapel of the Four,
> faded now almost to nothing. Tucked into a crack beneath it, folded tight against the
> weather, is a page written in a neat and steady hand.

**Sister Wenna**

> Seventh day. We have gone around the fields rather than through them, north toward the
> Rise, as the records say. There is a sickness here, and I no longer believe it is a
> natural one. It spreads from the great barn at the heart of the farmland, the way rot
> spreads from a wound.
>
> Brother Ansel wished to go inside and put an end to whatever is there. I forbade it. In
> the night, he went anyway. We waited until dawn. He did not come back. The Four keep him.
>
> I saw what he saw, before he went. Through the broken roof, deep in the web, a light
> like a green star. The oldest records in the Chapel speak of three stones that turn the
> key to the Chambers. The Four forgive me, I have begun to wonder whether we have just seen
> one of them, in the belly of that thing.
>
> We go on toward the Rise.

### Q2-N3 - A note for Tam

- **When:** the heroes search the Fenwick house in Millbrook. The note is nailed inside the
  cellar door, where only someone hiding down there would see it.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Hesta Fenwick.
- **Voice (Hesta):** a farm woman, frightened but holding it together for her son. Firm on
  "even if it's me."
- **Sound:** a door creaking on one hinge.

**Narrator**

> The small house at the end of the lane has been stripped by the threads, but the cellar
> door is still shut tight. Nailed to the inside of it, at the height of a child's eyes, is
> a scrap of paper in large, careful letters.

**Hesta Fenwick**

> Tam. Stay down here. Keep the door shut. There's bread and water on the shelf and you're
> to make it last.
>
> The Blightweaver is calling people to the barn. It calls in voices you know. Don't you go
> to the barn, no matter what you hear. Not even if it's me. Especially not if it's me.
>
> Someone will come. Stay hidden until they do.
>
> I love you. Ma.

### Q2-03 - The tithe barn

- **When:** the heroes first see into the Blightweaver's lair.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** building dread; the last line quick and sharp.
- **Sound:** creaking silk, a slow wet heartbeat.

> Every thread in the fields ends here.
>
> The old tithe barn has split open like an overripe gourd, its beams furred with gray
> silk. The web inside is thick enough to walk on, and it breathes. Shapes hang in it,
> wrapped tight and very still.
>
> At the heart of it all squats the Blightweaver. It is a bloated, many-legged mass the
> size of a hay cart, its skin stretched tight and glistening. Deep inside its swollen
> body, something glows a sickly green, like a lantern buried in rot.
>
> Its legs begin to twitch along the threads. It knows you are here.

### Q2-04 - The green stone

- **When:** the Blightweaver dies.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** grim, then quietly hopeful at the end. Clue 4: give the heartbeat a moment.
- **Sound:** a wet collapse; then a soft rain of falling threads.

> The Blightweaver shudders, sags, and bursts, spilling its foul insides across the floor.
>
> In the mess lies a stone the size of a hen's egg, glowing a pale and sickly green. When
> you pick it up, it is warm. And for a moment it seems to beat against your palm, slow
> and steady, like a heartbeat. Then it is still. Somewhere deep inside it, so faint you
> cannot be sure, something is wailing.
>
> Outside, a sound like rain begins. It is the webs. All across the fields, the gray
> threads are drying, snapping, and falling to the ground.
>
> The blight on the Eastmarch is broken.

### Q2-05 - Smoke on the hills

- **When:** the heroes reach the exit toward the Rise.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** steady; a warning in the last line.
- **Sound:** dead, still air; far-off drums.

> Beyond the last hedgerow, the land climbs toward a line of broken hills. The air is
> cleaner here, but not by much. As the stink of rot fades, the smell of smoke takes its
> place.
>
> On the highest hill you can make out the jagged shape of an old Warden fortress, and
> fires burning all around it. The Rise, where Sister Wenna's pilgrims were heading.
>
> The ogres are waiting.
