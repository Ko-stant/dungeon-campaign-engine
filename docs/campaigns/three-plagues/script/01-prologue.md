# The Three Plagues - Script: Prologue

**Last Updated**: 2026-09-29 14:39 EDT

Part of the read-aloud script; see [README.md](README.md) for the format and how to load it.
Everything above the `##` heading is left out when the script is assembled.

Played after the players have named their heroes and heard the house rules. The lord tells
them only what Haldmere knows: the land is dying, the Elemental Chambers were sealed long
ago, and every party sent east in ten years has failed. Soul Gems, plagues and bosses are
for the heroes to discover.

## Prologue

### P0-01 - Haldmere Cross

- **When:** the campaign's first scene, after the heroes are named and the rules are given.
- **Speakers:** Narrator, then Sergeant Corin Hale.
- **Voice (Narrator):** unhurried and observant; let each detail land. **Voice (Hale):**
  gruff, polite and tired; a soldier trying to sound more hopeful than he is.
- **Sound:** wagon wheels creaking to a stop, a horse snorting, then no wind at all; a
  single hammer somewhere, striking cold metal.

**Narrator**

> The wagon lurches to a halt, and the dust it kicked up along the road settles slowly
> around you. It has nowhere else to go. There is no wind here. None at all. The air hangs
> heavy and still, and it tastes of grit.
>
> This is Haldmere Cross. It must have been a fine market town once. The square is wide
> and well paved, ringed with tall timber houses and the painted signs of a dozen trades.
> But half the shutters are closed, and half the signs have faded past reading. At the well
> in the middle of the square, a line of townsfolk waits with buckets, and the water that
> comes up is the colour of rust. Beyond the rooftops, the sails of a windmill stand
> perfectly still against a colourless sky. In the doorway of a smithy, a broad-shouldered
> smith stares down at a bed of coals that should be glowing, and is not.
>
> One of you presses a few coins into the driver's hand. He takes them without a word,
> turns his wagon around in the square, and whips the horses back the way you came. He
> does not look back. Whatever this place is, he has no wish to stay in it a moment longer
> than he must.
>
> People are watching you. Not openly. From behind half-closed shutters, from the line at
> the well, from the shadow of the smithy door. Then a man in a dented breastplate and a
> faded blue surcoat crosses the square towards you, one hand raised in greeting. He is not
> young, and he looks as though he has not slept well in a very long time.

**Sergeant Hale**

> Welcome to Haldmere. And thank you, all of you, for answering the call. I'm Sergeant
> Corin Hale, of the Keep guard.
>
> Lord Voss is eager to meet you. I'm sorry to rush you. I'm sure you'd give a great deal
> for a hot meal and a bed at the Tollen Arms, and you'll have both, I promise. But the lord
> asked me to bring you straight to him, and time is one thing we don't have much of.
>
> This way, if you please.

### P0-02 - The road to Voss Keep

- **When:** straight after P0-01.
- **Speaker:** Narrator.
- **Voice:** steady and a little sombre; slow down for the garden.
- **Sound:** footsteps on dry gravel, a crow, the scrape of iron bolts.

> Your party nods, and follows Sergeant Hale out of the square and up the winding road
> towards the hill where the lord's house stands.
>
> Along the way, the town shows you more of itself. A baker's stall with three small loaves
> on it, and a line of people waiting for them. A child tipping a rain barrel to reach the
> last of the water at the bottom. Faces that turn to watch you pass, hollow-eyed and
> quiet. Nobody calls out. Nobody asks who you are. They have watched strangers ride east
> before, and they have learned not to hope.
>
> Voss Keep stands at the top of the road, a squat grey house of stone behind an old
> curtain wall. Two guards stand at its gate, spears held a little too tightly. Sergeant
> Hale calls out to them, and they hurry to draw the bolts. As you pass between them,
> neither one will meet your eyes.
>
> Inside the wall there was a garden once, and a proud one. Rows of fruit trees. Trimmed
> hedges. A fountain carved as four figures holding up a single basin: one crowned with
> stone, one with a wave, one with a feather, one with a flame. The trees are bare now,
> their leaves curled brown on the ground. The hedges have gone grey and brittle. The
> fountain is dry, and a crack runs through all four of the carved figures.
>
> Sergeant Hale keeps you moving. Across the courtyard, through a pair of heavy doors, along
> a hall hung with faded tapestries, until at last he stops before a tall oak door, and
> knocks twice.

### P0-03 - The lord's request

- **When:** the heroes enter Lord Voss's audience chamber.
- **Speakers:** Lord Aldren Voss and the Narrator, taking turns.
- **Voice (Voss):** late fifties, deep, tired but proud; plain words, no speeches. He is
  asking, not ordering. **Voice (Narrator):** quiet, filling the pauses.
- **Sound:** a small fire, a clock ticking, silence at the windows.
- **Note:** any narrator break is a good place to stop for the players' questions.

**Lord Voss**

> Come in, come in, please. Sergeant, close the door on your way out, would you? I would
> rather my household did not hear this.

**Narrator**

> The door clicks shut behind you. Lord Aldren Voss is a tall man gone thin, grey at the
> temples, in a coat that was made for someone heavier. The chamber is lined with shelves of
> ledgers and old maps, and a small fire burns in the grate. It gives light, but almost no
> warmth. The lord gestures you towards the chairs by the fire, but he does not sit.

**Lord Voss**

> Thank you for heeding my call. I know how far some of you have come.
>
> You came through Haldmere Cross to get here, so you have seen it for yourselves. This
> country is dying. The wells run low, and what water they give tastes of iron. The wind
> has not turned a mill sail in two seasons. The forges that made Haldmere Cross the finest
> market in the east have gone cold. The smiths feed them every scrap of coal they have,
> and still something draws the heat away, as if the fire itself were being drunk. And now
> the soil in our fields comes up grey behind the plough.
>
> My grandfather called this the richest vale in the kingdom. If nothing changes, my
> grandchildren, if they live, will know it as a grave.

**Narrator**

> He falls quiet, and crosses to the window. Out beyond the town, the sails of the windmill
> hang motionless. For a long moment he says nothing at all. His hand tightens on the
> window ledge. When he turns back to you, he looks older than he did a moment ago.

**Lord Voss**

> My archivist, Brother Tobias, believes he has found the reason. He has spent the better
> part of a year in the undercroft of the Chapel of the Four, among records nobody has read
> in generations.
>
> Long ago, this country was kept by the Wardens. They watched over the Elemental Chambers,
> somewhere deep in the east, beyond Tollen Hill. Whatever power lay in those Chambers
> flowed out into this land. It kept our fields full, our wells sweet and our forges
> burning. It made Haldmere what it was.
>
> No one has seen a Warden in over a century. And no one has set foot in the Chambers for
> longer than anyone can remember. The last record Tobias could find says only that they
> were sealed. Whatever they gave us, it seems, was sealed away with them. And ever since,
> this land has been dying. Slowly, at first. Not slowly any longer.

**Narrator**

> He rubs his eyes. When he speaks again, his voice is lower.

**Lord Voss**

> I have not simply sat and watched it happen. For ten years I have sent people east.
> Soldiers. Hunters. Sellswords who were paid far more than they were worth. Priests from
> the Chapel. Most of them never came back. The few who did were changed. They would not
> speak of what they had seen, not to me, not to their own families. One of them has not
> spoken a single word since the day he returned.
>
> And it is getting worse. The farms of the Eastmarch have gone silent. The grain carts
> stopped coming this spring. The last drovers to make it home swore they were set upon by
> ogres, great brutes who took every sack of grain and every head of cattle, and would have
> taken the drovers too, had they been any slower.
>
> Whatever has done this has made its home somewhere beyond the old Warden gatehouse under
> Tollen Hill. The townsfolk call it the Crumbling Halls now, because the whole place looks
> ready to come down on anyone foolish enough to go inside. It is the only road east.
>
> I cannot spare any more of my people. And if I am honest, I do not think any of them
> would go.

**Narrator**

> He looks at each of you in turn. And then the lord of Haldmere does something you did not
> expect. He bows his head.

**Lord Voss**

> So I am asking you. Please. Go east, through the Halls. Find out what has become of the
> Elemental Chambers. And whatever evil has made its home out there, drive it from this
> land.
>
> Haldmere has little left to offer. But whatever it has is yours, if you can give it back
> its future.

### P0-04 - At the gate

- **When:** the heroes reach the doors of the Crumbling Halls (right before Q1-01).
- **Speaker:** Maren Ashby.
- **Voice:** brisk and practical; she has done this before and did not like how it went.
- **Sound:** dead, still air; a low groan of settling stone.

> This is as far as the road goes. Past these doors it's Warden stone all the way to the
> east side of the hill. Or it was.
>
> Listen. Hear that? That's the hill settling. It does it more every week.
>
> Lord Voss had me pack what we could spare. It isn't much, so don't waste it.
>
> One more thing. The last patrol we sent in came out with three men instead of five, and
> none of them would say what they saw. Keep your torches lit and your voices low. If you
> find anything the others left behind, a pack, a letter, anything, bring it back. Their
> families deserve to know. And when you find the far gate, don't look back. Just keep
> going east.
